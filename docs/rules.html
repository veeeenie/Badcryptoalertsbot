Rule Book

The trader's rules, set 23–24 October 2026; risk changed to 3% of the portfolio on 30 October. New rules get added one at a time, by decision.

  1. The trend is our friend.Trade only in the direction of the weekly trend. The weekly EMAs (25/50/100, or 25/50 for coins under 100 weeks) and the weekly structure (lower highs and lower lows, or higher highs and higher lows) must agree.
  2. Long timeframes come first.The weekly leads and the daily supports; no dithering on the daily, and nothing below it.
  3. Entry, stop and take-profit all sit at supply and demand regions.Never ad hoc. A zone is the base before a strong weekly move. The stop is backed by that structure and never moved to fit the entry.
  4. Let the trade come to us.Orders wait at the region; we don't chase price.
  5. Don't force the trade.No asymmetric higher-timeframe setup means stay in cash. The gate: at least 1.4 : 1 with all orders filled, and with only the first order filled.
  6. Nothing gets in without a stop-loss and a take-profit.The stop goes in with the entry; the take-profit is placed once in the trade.
  7. Enter and exit in tranches.Three entry orders at fixed levels across the zone (near edge, middle, far edge), 30 : 30 : 40, growing toward the best price. They never follow price: a level price has already passed is dropped, not bought or sold at market. Price beyond the zone's far edge means wait for a daily close back inside. Two equal exits, each 0.5% in front of the next opposite zones.
  8. Risk 3% of the portfolio per trade.The loss if every order fills and the stop is hit (about $3.70 on a $122 portfolio; it moves with the portfolio, recalculated every hour). A wider stop means a smaller size. Risk never goes above 3%: when 3% can't fund three orders of at least $10 (Hyperliquid's minimum), the plan uses two orders (45 : 55 at the near and far edge), then one; if even one order is under $10, there's no trade.
  9. Never get liquidated.The stop always comes before liquidation; leverage is chosen so liquidation sits at least twice as far as the stop.
  10. The stop sits beyond the level where most of the rejections cluster, with breathing room.A quarter of a weekly ATR beyond it. Not beyond a lone wick, and never so close that a normal sweep takes it out before the move begins.
  11. Entries sit where price is likely to reach them.So the position isn't left too light if the last order doesn't fill.
  12. Account size decides how many trades.Everything trades from one wallet (about $122 on 30 Oct 2026), so 2–4 ideas at a time.
